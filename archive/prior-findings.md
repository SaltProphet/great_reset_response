# Previous research synthesis (not a verbatim transcript)

1. A technically open internet can coexist with de facto restrictions on participation in essential services.
2. Authentication and identity systems are distinct from central biometric storage; practical service access depends on alternatives and appeals.
3. User's AI-token comparison concerns metered, account-linked work allocation and performance measurement—not a literal token replacement for money.
4. Algorithmic labor-management systems can combine location flexibility with monitoring and suspension risk.
5. WEF Great Reset and earlier digital-currency/identity initiatives are public, long-range institutional proposals.
6. WEF involvement in a proposal is different from proof that it caused a government's implementation.
7. BIS wholesale programmable settlement is distinct from controls on retail purchases.
8. Documented reversals, non-discrimination safeguards, unlaunched pilots and independent alternatives must remain in the analysis.
9. PHP2Shell produced operational disruption reports in banks and emergency alerts; outage does not equal compromise.
10. The central research test is whether independently operating identity, payment and employment systems develop actual shared eligibility decisions.
